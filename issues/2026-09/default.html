The Roundup
This month’s research is centered on Contact Form, EDC Blueprint Page, Solution Page Campaign Mode, IMM Team Newsletter. The strongest value is decision-shaping rather than broad launch approval: each track points to a clearer question about what users understand, trust, or know how to do next.
Research Findings
01
CONTACT FORM
High confidence

The newest Contact Form redesign (V3) is the clearest win this program has produced on this page: consolidating the form, sharpening the page's layout hierarchy, and simplifying the visual treatment — lighter Cloud White tones and plain fills in place of bordered panels — together lifted sentiment sharply without costing comprehension or engagement. Source deck ↗

EVIDENCE
Sentiment
84%
▲ from 39% baseline
Overall UX
74%
▲ from 55% baseline
Comprehension
82%
▲ from 76% baseline
Engagement
60%
▲ from 36% baseline

V3 also cut "Overwhelming" 16%→5% and "Complicated" 11%→9%, while "Helpful" held steady (57%→55%) — the gains aren't coming at the cost of feeling less helpful. (Earlier rounds drew complaints like "I'm not sure how they can help"; V3 is what resolved that.)

DIRECTION

Ship V3 as the new baseline for Contact Form, and use its full pattern — the consolidated form, the sharpened layout hierarchy, and the Cloud White / fills-not-borders treatment — as the reference for the next round of utility-page redesigns, rather than treating each page as a one-off.

02
EDC BLUEPRINT PAGE
High confidence

The newest Enterprise Data Cloud (EDC) Blueprint page (live Sept 16) delivered the program's biggest sentiment and comprehension gains on this page — but it did so by reverting to a broader, less focused set of calls-to-action, and Intent dropped back to its worst level as a result.

Respondent quote (an earlier round): “Why would you assume I'd start an assessment straight away?” Source deck ↗

EVIDENCE
Sentiment
83%
▲ from 26% baseline
26% → 29% → 37% → 83%
Comprehension
79%
Program-best
Intent
42%
▼ from 69% V2, best round

Intent fell once the page split attention across six competing next steps (Run a Workshop, Start Your Assessment, Get the Blueprint Guide, and more) instead of V2's single dominant "Start Your Assessment" path.

DIRECTION

Restore "Start Your Assessment" as the single primary CTA — the structure that won in V2 — rather than splitting attention across six options. Keep "What is an EDC?" as a secondary route in the hero rather than removing it: respondents who don't recognize the category term want that escape hatch, and losing it would trade one confusion for another. Test that combination next round to confirm it recovers Intent without giving back the sentiment and comprehension gains. Separately, keep leading with a plain-language, outcome-first statement: "Enterprise Data Cloud" still reads as unexplained marketing jargon to first-time visitors, independent of the CTA problem.

03
SOLUTION PAGE CAMPAIGN MODE
High confidence

The VMware-ad-driven Solution Page campaign keeps its promise to visitors — but converts them toward the wrong next step: the page is understood and trusted, yet doesn't clearly guide people toward the specific challenge that brought them there. Source deck ↗

EVIDENCE
Met or exceeded expectations
73%
Felt let down
9%
Found it educational
25%
Found their challenge
25%

Desirability (81/100) and comprehension (76%) are both strong — the page keeps the ad's promise — but the low educational and challenge-findability numbers are exactly why the team's call was "Refine Design," not "Implement."

DIRECTION

The page's conversion path needs to be rebuilt around the demo — the thing visitors already trust — rather than the generic form driving the page today, so a strong first impression converts into the right next step instead of any next step.

04
IMM TEAM NEWSLETTER
High confidence

Everpure's monthly customer newsletter — built by the Integrated Marketing team — didn't clearly read as a newsletter to a fifth of the proxy panel that reviewed it in testing. That panel isn't confirmed to overlap with actual Everpure customers, so this is best read as a behavioral pattern worth addressing, not a verdict delivered by customers themselves.

EVIDENCE
Read it as a regular newsletter
33%
Said "nothing in particular" or "felt like spam"
20%

The send currently mixes two goals — driving a specific action (e.g., event signups) and surfacing consumable content — without being explicit about which each section serves. That structural ambiguity is a plausible driver of the "felt like spam" reaction on its own, independent of whether this particular panel matches the real customer base.

DIRECTION

Don't make further copy tweaks until the newsletter's core business objective (inform vs. drive a specific action) is explicitly defined — then build a genuinely reworked design variant around that single objective, not word-level edits, and put it in front of respondents. Treat this as a hypothesis to address rather than a customer mandate, and validate with an actual customer sample if one becomes available. An unsent test variant costs nothing to run.

Meaningful Comparisons
01
VIDEO & DEMO SECTION NAMING
Medium confidence

Five candidate names were tested for the video/demo section of the site — Everpure Play, Everpure Studio, EverpureTV, Everpure Demos, and Pure360 — and the team is moving forward with "Everpure Demos." Source deck ↗

CRITERIA

The name was chosen for consistency rather than a comprehension or preference score: it directly matches the site's existing "Watch Demos" call-to-action and the everpuredata.com/demos URL, keeping visitors on one consistent term rather than introducing a new one for the same destination.

DIRECTION

This closes out the naming decision — treat "Everpure Demos" as final for this section unless a future round reopens it.

What Is Still Unresolved
01
IMM Team Newsletter
Newsletter redesign

Does defining a single business objective and building a wholesale redesign around it actually reduce the 20% who see this as spam or unclear, or does the newsletter's underlying two-goals-in-one structure need to change first?

02
EDC Blueprint Page
CTA focus vs. visual refinement

Does re-applying V2's single-CTA structure to the Sept 16 Live visual design recover Intent while keeping the sentiment and comprehension gains, or does fixing one regress the other?

03
Contact Form
Design-language generalization

Does the V3 treatment produce the same sentiment lift on other utility pages, or is Contact Form's simpler task uniquely suited to it?

Recommended Actions
01
Contact Form

Contact Form: Ship V3 to production this cycle, and add its full pattern — form consolidation, layout hierarchy, and Cloud White / fills-not-borders treatment — to the shared pattern library so it's the default starting point for the next utility-page redesign brief, not a page-specific one-off.

02
EDC Blueprint Page

EDC Blueprint Page: Prototype the Sept 16 Live visual design with "Start Your Assessment" restored as the single primary hero CTA and "What is an EDC?" kept as a secondary route, then run it as a validation test next round to confirm Intent recovers without losing the sentiment/comprehension gains.

03
Solution Page Campaign Mode

Solution Page Campaign Mode: Prototype and test the demo-gated form (V1) against the merged "guide + demo" option before the next campaign cycle.

04
IMM Team Newsletter

IMM Team Newsletter: Define the newsletter's core business objective first, then prototype a couple of alternate layouts for the monthly send and run them in front of respondents as design variants — treating this as a behavioral pattern to fix rather than a confirmed customer complaint, since the reviewing panel wasn't confirmed as actual Everpure customers.

05
Video & Demo Section Naming

Video & Demo Section Naming: Roll out "Everpure Demos" across the section's label, page title, and internal links, and confirm it doesn't conflict with the existing "Watch Demos" CTA copy.

This cycle's clearest lesson: visual refinement — lighter Cloud White tones, borders replaced with fills — is paying off in sentiment and comprehension wherever it's tested. But it only pays off cleanly when it's layered onto an already-proven, focused call-to-action; the one page that reverted to a broader menu of options while refining its visuals gave back its Intent gains in the process.
