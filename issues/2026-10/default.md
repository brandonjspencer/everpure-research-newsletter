Two habits ran through October's tests. People go by plain, familiar words, not our labels: buyers will likely confuse “remanufactured” with “refurbished,” and renaming the menu changed how people describe Everpure, not what they could find. And people look around before they commit: when the Contact page dropped its chat card, nearly half of first clicks went to the site menu. All four findings carry Medium confidence.

## Research Findings

### Platform Menu

We tested four versions of the site's “Our Platform” menu, including a rename to “Data Management.” The name made no detectable difference to what people could find (44% vs. 46% found storage as a service), and only 10–12% called Everpure a platform under any name. What it changes is how people describe Everpure afterward (more “data management,” less “storage”), so the choice is a positioning call, not a usability fix.

> Respondent quote (asked whether the “Our Platform” menu matched what they expected): “expected detailed data infrastructure, this is marketing overview.”

#### Evidence

- **Overall UX: 70%** (vs. 55–58% “Our Platform” (test score))
- **Sentiment: 91%** (vs. 46–61% “Our Platform” (test score))
- **Found storage as a service: 44%** (vs. 46% on an otherwise identical “Our Platform” menu)
- **Called Everpure a platform: 10–12%** (Under every name, incl. “Our Platform”)

The overall lead is liking, not use: expectations (73–74%) and success (40–46%) barely move, and 12% vs. 20% picked a negative word, which the researchers call directional. Asked what Everpure sells, 52% said data management under “Data Management,” but 38% said storage (56–76% elsewhere).

[Source deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1pctdDZGch1IiQSy6D5YMs0yho81yuLDeXyQAt2QzDCg/edit)

#### Direction

We're moving forward with testing one more variant that keeps the platform message forward: a merged “Platform & Products” menu, with the data-management story still present but not the hero, in its own “AI Data Readiness” menu. Until it reads out, the trade-off stands: “Data Management” owns the data-management story at the cost of fewer people saying “storage,” and if “Our Platform” stays, list the Enterprise Data Cloud (EDC) item first rather than “AI Data Ready” (46% vs. 38% found storage as a service).

#### Confidence

Medium confidence

### Remanufactured Storage Program

Everpure sells remanufactured storage systems — rebuilt, tested and certified, and backed by Everpure support — for less than new. We showed buyers similar programs from Cisco, HPE and IBM, and asked a separate group where they'd look for Everpure's. Trust followed the maker's warranty, but buyers will likely confuse “remanufactured” with “refurbished,” and when we showed them today's menu without the link, they headed to Products and few could tell where the program would live.

> Respondent quote: “I’d call it certified refurbished enterprise storage.”

#### Evidence

- **Called it “refurbished” in their own words: 36%** (vs. 4% “remanufactured”)
- **Trusted “Certified Refurbished” most: 36%** (vs. 9% “Certified Remanufactured”)
- **Chose the FlashArray row: 4%** (58% opened Products first (today's menu, no link))
- **Want price, performance & warranty vs. new: ~3 in 4** (Before asking for a quote)

The warranty earned the trust: HPE's “Certified Quality” drew 39% of trust clicks, Cisco's “same warranty and support” 22%, IBM's card none. Listing them beside new ones doesn't hurt (49% think more of new models, 2% less), and 71–79% picked browsing or reading as their next step (5–18% sales).

[Source deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1pctdDZGch1IiQSy6D5YMs0yho81yuLDeXyQAt2QzDCg/edit)

#### Direction

Keep “remanufactured” — it's what the program is — but support buyers' discovery intent: use the word they search with (“refurbished”), plus the terms rival programs use (“certified pre-owned,” “renewed”), in page copy, search terms and the menu line's description, then explain the difference plainly near the top: what remanufactured means for Everpure systems and what the warranty and support cover. Once Product confirms the warranty and support terms, build Phase 1: a named line in the Products menu, a warranty-and-support section, FAQs on price, performance, warranty and testing before the quote button, and the systems shown beside new ones.

#### Confidence

Medium confidence

### Contact Form

Last month's winning Contact page (V3) put a chat card above the form. Its follow-up (V4) led with the form and moved chat into a floating AI assistant in the corner. People understood it as well but engaged less: the chat card had been the way to look around without committing, and without it nearly half of first clicks went to the site menu, not the form.

#### Evidence

- **First clicks to the site menu: 48.5%** (▲ from 35.6% V3)
- **Said they'd use the form: 40%** (vs. 26% on V3 — stated, not clicks)
- **AI assistant first clicks: 2 of 101** (None on its “Speak now” button)
- **V3 chat-card first clicks: 30%** (The demand the card was absorbing)

Test scores agree: comprehension held level with V3, but engagement fell to 44 from 58 for V3 in the same round. Site-menu clicks were led by “Products” and “Start Here,” “Watch a Demo” took 16% (its series high), and 26% still said chat is how they'd reach out.

[Source deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1s2I6RlFZPT50pGSDKvSt2GhBmADcG5LSXW8A9BALZVA/edit)

#### Direction

Keep V4's form-first structure for its clarity, but give back a way to look around: put a “Not ready? Explore the platform / Watch a demo” block where V3's chat card was, and test it as the next version. Lean into the demo path; it's where hesitant visitors actually go.

#### Confidence

Medium confidence

### Newsroom

Everpure's newsroom draws the highest Intent of the five newsrooms tested and already reads as made for IT and business leaders. Its comprehension is the lowest, but that gap is mostly awareness: Everpure is a new name, and Apple and Spotify get that background for free. The page doesn't close it — the top doesn't say who Everpure is or which story matters most, and there's no visible way to find news by topic.

> Respondent quote: “It is the news page of Everpure which I am not sure what it is.”

#### Evidence

- **Intent (test score): 63%** (Highest of the five)
- **Comprehension: 68%** (Lowest of five; Apple and Spotify 79%)
- **Said finding a topic would be easy: 52%** (vs. 82% on Spotify's single row of topics)
- **Featured content's share of clicks: 48.9%** (All clicks, live-site analytics, Feb 23–Sep 30)

13% didn't understand the page (Apple 2%), and fewer would come back (55% vs. 67–71% for Apple and Spotify). First clicks split across featured news (20%), the banner (18%) and the video (17%), and Microsoft's long stack of topic sections felt overwhelming to 33%.

[Source deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1pctdDZGch1IiQSy6D5YMs0yho81yuLDeXyQAt2QzDCg/edit)

#### Direction

Refine the top of the page around three things. Make search visible: PR reports journalists use archival search to compare new stories with older ones, so it stays. Give featured stories more weight and hierarchy — for example, a supporting visual for the lead story — since featured content already draws nearly half of all clicks. And find the right number of topic categories: enough to browse by topic, today's biggest gap, without becoming Microsoft's long stack. Add one plain line saying who Everpure is, keep the press-release archive one click away, then test against this baseline.

#### Confidence

Medium confidence

## Meaningful Comparisons

### AI Data Readiness Page

We tested three headlines for the top of the AI Data Readiness page: a general one (“Build the data foundation AI depends on”), a data-sovereignty angle and a cost-and-supply-chain angle. The team kept the general headline as the default; the open decision is whether a plain-language rewrite of an angle can match it.

#### Criteria

- **Picked the right challenge: 69%** (General headline; 53% and 33% for the angles)
- **Called it “Simple”: 43%** (vs. 25–32% for the angles)
- **Wanted more information: 64–65%** (Flat across all three)
- **First clicks on Watch Video: 55%** (General page)

Judge a rewrite on understanding, not interest, which stayed flat. It should match the general page on picking the challenge the page is about and on feeling simple. Under “Efficiency is architecture,” only 33% picked cost and 19% didn't understand what the platform does (8–10% on the others).

[Source deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1s2I6RlFZPT50pGSDKvSt2GhBmADcG5LSXW8A9BALZVA/edit)

#### Direction

Put the video and demo up front now, and run a plain-language cost headline, without “architecture,” head-to-head against the general one.

#### Confidence

Medium confidence

### Video and Homepage

After a 30-second video ad, people landed on one of three Everpure home pages — the full page, a shorter one and the simplest — and all three felt like the right place to land: 83–90% said the page matched what the ad promised. Confidence is high on that landing-page match, which held on all three pages; what needs discipline now is the call-to-action labels.

#### Criteria

- **Said the page matched the ad: 83–90%** (Across all three home pages)
- **Understood the ad: 85%** (Top two ratings)
- **Read the ad as about storage: 32%** (61% read it as protection or security)
- **Repeated the ad's actual claim: 4%** (93% picked a positive word)

Judge each call-to-action on whether its label says plainly what happens next, not on how much people like the page: people like the ad, but only a third read it as being about storage and 4% could repeat its claim.

[Source deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1s2I6RlFZPT50pGSDKvSt2GhBmADcG5LSXW8A9BALZVA/edit)

#### Direction

Ship the chosen landing page, but lock the CTA labels first: replace “End the Data Siege” on the orange button with a plain label that says what happens next (or make “Explore Our Platform” the main one), add one line saying what Everpure is and who it's for, and move the demo higher.

#### Confidence

High confidence

## What Is Still Unresolved

- **Platform Menu** — Next variant: “Platform & Products”: Would a merged “Platform & Products” menu that leads with the platform story — and lists storage, capabilities and software by name — keep the findability lead of this round's merged “Platform” menu, which led with storage (58% found storage, vs. 38–46%), without its negative reactions (28%)? And would giving the data-management story its own “AI Data Readiness” menu keep it present without making it the hero? We're testing both now.
- **Contact Form** — AI assistant role: Is there still a job for chat? 26% say it's how they'd reach out, yet the corner AI assistant caught 2 of 101 first clicks. Moving it into the page body and re-testing would show whether it can catch them or should stay background help.
- **NetApp Comparison Page** — Comparison credibility: Most people found the page making the case for switching from NetApp clear and credible, but its table shows only Everpure's column on screen, and one in five called it “Generic.” Once NetApp's side is visible and it has one clear next step, does that share fall? We're testing the latest update this week to see how much it moves the needle.

## Recommended Actions

- **Platform Menu:** Read the “Platform & Products” variant out against this round before settling the menu name, and rewrite each item's description to say plainly what it is either way — “marketing copy” was the most common complaint.
- **Remanufactured Storage Program:** Test a Products-menu line and hero that keep “Certified Remanufactured” but pair it with buyers' own words and a one-line explanation of the difference, aiming for half of people to find the line, and bring two or three ways of showing price against new to Legal.
- **Contact Form:** Test V4 plus the “Not ready?” block next round, judged on whether it wins back V3's look-around clicks (30% on the chat card) while keeping V4's form preference (40%), and report that 40% as stated intent, not form fills.
- **Newsroom:** Once the site migration settles (around November), prototype the new top — visible search, a weightier lead story with a supporting visual, and a short topic row — and test it against this baseline, judged on comprehension (68% now), ease of finding a topic (52%; Spotify 82%) and whether people would come back (55%; Apple 67%, Spotify 71%).
- **AI Data Readiness Page:** Move the video and demo up front now, and book the plain-language cost headline against the general one for the next round.
